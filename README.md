#How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating “user stories” help with this?
My process starts with engaging the stakeholder directly rather than guessing. As a Product Owner for the SNHU Travel Project, this meant using interviews, email, surveys, and focus groups in order to gather data and build the program from that. 
Creating user stories allows me to see client features, the scope of the project, and the priority it needs to be on the Product Backlog. When I had doubts about a feature that was requested, I would email the client to clarify what exactly was being asked.

#How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?
Having played Scrum Master, Product Owner, Developer, and Tester across this project gave me the ability to have a working process instead of just a textbook list. As Scrum Master I was in charge of Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Backlog Refinement, Sprint Review, and Sprint Retrospective. For example, Backlog Refinement allowed me to reprioritize the backlog, keeping our priorities current. This ensured we, in different job roles, stayed focused on our end goal, getting the job “done.” What I want to incorporate into my future development work is the ability to adapt to the quick change just as we did with the SNHU Travel Program. Mid-Sprint we were tasked with changing our main subject from Top 5 Destinations to Top Wellness/Detox vacations. This required steering our project midway through, and we had to reprioritize some items on our backlog in order for us to finish the project with the same deadline as initially given. 

#What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?
A good team member in software development is someone who has the project as their top priority. This means that we follow the Rules of Behavior just as we did with our team’s charter, “change over following a plan,” which in practice means acting as servant leaders in our roles, allowing us to build upon each other’s work instead of fighting it. An example of this is when we had our estimation discussion, we responded to our classmate’s choice by suggesting Affinity Grouping as a complementary practice rather than a competing one. This is similar to reprioritizing a backlog instead of arguing about it. At the end of the day, it’s not about arguing a point, but rather keeping the team moving forward, together.
